Most hot and cold water systems in the UK and Ireland rely on temperature, rather than chemicals, to control Legionella. The principle is simple: keep cold water cold and hot water hot, so the bacteria never get the warm, still water they need to multiply. The detail is in the numbers.

## 20°C - the cold water ceiling

Cold water should be stored and distributed below 20°C. In practice this is measured at the sentinel outlets after running the tap for up to two minutes. Cold water creeping above 20°C is common in summer, in plant rooms and on risers that share a duct with heating pipework, and it is one of the most frequent reasons we recommend insulation or re-routing.

## 45°C - the growth zone

Between roughly 20°C and 45°C Legionella can multiply, and it does so fastest around body temperature. This is the band we are trying to keep water out of. Every remedial action in a written scheme comes back to this range.

## 50°C - the hot water outlet minimum

Hot water should reach 50°C at the outlet within one minute of running. In healthcare premises the figure is 55°C, because the guidance recognises a more vulnerable population. Where scalding is a risk, thermostatic mixing valves are fitted close to the outlet, and the 50°C reading is taken upstream of the valve.

## 55°C - the return

On systems with a hot water circulation loop, the return to the calorifier should be at least 50°C, and many schemes set 55°C as their target to give a margin. A cool return is the classic sign of a loop that has been extended over the years without rebalancing.

## 60°C - the calorifier

Hot water should be stored at 60°C or above. Readings are taken at the flow from the calorifier. A calorifier that is stored below 60°C, or that stratifies with a cool layer at the base, needs attention.

## Why the monthly readings matter

Monthly sentinel temperatures and annual readings at a representative sample of other outlets are the evidence that your scheme is working. Done badly, they are a box-ticking exercise. Done well, they give you an early warning: a cold outlet drifting from 17°C to 19°C to 21°C over a summer is a story, and a good responsible person reads it before the sampling results do.
